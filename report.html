I.The doctrine and its sources

Every negotiation over Jerusalem eventually collides with a doctrine that most Western diplomats have never read: the principle that land once held by Muslims may never be permanently relinquished to non-Muslim rule. This report traces that principle to its sources, documents how it is taught and invoked today, and shows what it means for any proposed compromise over the Holy City.

The doctrine is not a fringe position. It appears in rulings, sermons, and official statements cited throughout this report — and it functions as a standing veto on permanence: agreements may be accepted, but only as pauses, never as endings.

“The dispute is treated as theological, not territorial — which is why offers of territory alone have never resolved it.”

Chapter I collects the primary sources. Chapters II and III follow the doctrine from the page into policy, and into the specific case of Jerusalem — where its consequences are most visible and least discussed.

Sources: every claim in this report is footnoted to a primary source — rulings, published statements, and official documents — listed in the appendix and linked in the web edition, so readers, journalists, and courts can verify each one directly.
